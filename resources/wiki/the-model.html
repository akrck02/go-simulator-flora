🌳 The flora model

Forests do not appear all at once. On bare ground, grasses and gorse arrive first; then trees that love the sun, like birch and pine; and, slowly, under their shade, the trees that can wait in the dark take over. A storm or a fire opens a clearing and the story starts again there. The flora plugin plays that story, year by year, for every species you give it, wherever the climate and the land let it live.

It knows nothing about any world: the species, their traits and their habitats come from the scenario. This page explains what happens in each cell, every year.

The pictures on this page come from real runs of the plugin, drawn in 2D from the fields it published: the example mountain, and small made-up fields that show one thing each (how they were made).

The landscape

The landscape is a grid over the climate’s extent, with cells of cell km (default: the temperature field’s). At the start the plugin samples, at every cell centre, the fields of the world: temperature and precipitation (from the climate plugin), elevation and slope (from the terrain plugin) bilinearly, and biome by the nearest cell. Land is every cell with a biome (or above sea level when there is no biome field). The names of the biomes come from the climate’s biomes event.

In every land cell, every species has:

  • its cover: the share of the ground its plants take, 0 to 1;
  • its saplings: the share of the ground with young plants waiting for room (the advance regeneration of LANDIS);
  • how many years it has grown there, which decides when it bears seeds.

Habitat

A species’ habitat is an envelope over the facts of a cell (bioclimatic envelope models, Busby 1991), written like go-simulator’s conditions:

habitat:
  temperature: {min: 9, max: 18}        # °C, the yearly mean
  precipitation: {min: 900}             # mm a year
  elevation: {max: 1500}                # metres
  slope: {max: 0.8}                     # rise over run
  biome: [riverbank]                    # one of the climate's biomes
  distance_to_old_oak: {max: 60}        # metres from an anchor of the rules file

Inside every range the habitat suits the species fully. Past an edge it fades linearly to nothing over the fact’s softness (softness: {temperature: 1.5, precipitation: 150, slope: 0.1, distance: 40}, in each fact’s unit; without one, the edge is hard). Values that are not ranges hold or not. The suitability of a cell is the product of every fact’s: 1 where everything suits, 0 where anything is out of reach. It is worked out once, at the start.

A made-up strip from 16 °C in the west to 0 °C in the east. Each tree grows where its range is; a plant dies faster where the habitat suits it only in part, so where two ranges meet only at their soft edges neither tree closes the forest, and the meadow keeps the gap.

Layers and light

Light is what plants compete for. Every species has a form: tree, shrub or herb. Trees shade shrubs, shrubs shade herbs.

  • The light that reaches a layer through the layers above it is 1 − 0.9 × min(1, their cover).
  • A species needs light by its shade tolerance (1 to 5): need = (5 − tolerance) / 4 × 0.8, so tolerance 5 grows in deep shade and tolerance 1 needs 80 % of full light. With some light it keeps (light − need) / (1 − need) of its growth, none below what it needs.

One year

This is the heart of the model. For every land cell and species, from the state at the start of the year:

  1. Seeds. Mature plants (grown at least maturity years in the cell) spread seeds around them with a Gaussian kernel as wide as their dispersal (metres); background_seeds reach every cell from far away (birds, storms). Species with spreads: false never seed.
  2. Saplings. Seeds become saplings where the habitat suits the species and there is light for them, both in the gaps of their layer and under its plants:
    • in the gaps, with the light the taller layers leave, less the shade of the plants around the gap: tall trees shade a small gap (0.6 when their layer is closed), shrubs less (0.3), grasses not at all; the ground a disturbance cleared gets nearly full light;
    • under the plants of their own layer, with the light that reaches the ground.

saplings += growth × seeds × suitability × (light in gaps + light under plants) × (1 − saplings); then 10 % of the saplings die waiting, more where the habitat does not suit.

  1. Recruitment. Saplings take the room plants left in their layer: recruited = min(saplings, growth × saplings × room × suitability × light from above).
  2. Death. Plants die of age (1 / longevity a year), of a habitat that does not suit them (up to 20 % a year) and of shade too deep for them (up to 10 % a year).
  3. Room. If the species of a layer together grew past the ground there is, they share it out in proportion.
  4. Disturbance. With disturbance.chance a year, a cell loses severity of its trees and shrubs (windthrow, fire) and they start again; herbs and saplings stay.

How a forest spreads

Seeds only come from mature plants, and they land around them as far as the species’ dispersal. Every plant that grows from them bears seeds in its turn once it is mature, so a species advances as a front, faster the farther its seeds fly and the sooner it matures.

Birch (dispersal 400 m, seeds from year 8) against oak (60 m, from year 40), from one planted tree each, with no seeds from far away. Birch, which needs full light, only fills about a third of the ground: it regenerates only where its own cover leaves enough light.

Succession

So pioneers that need full light take open ground first and cannot follow themselves under their own shade, while shade-tolerant species waiting below take over as the pioneers age: succession. Frequent disturbances keep the pioneers in the landscape; rare ones let the tolerant species close the forest. A light-demanding species forms open woodland, because it only regenerates where its own cover leaves enough light.

A made-up field where every species could live everywhere, from bare ground and without disturbances: meadow, then birch, then oak; beech, the most shade-tolerant and slowest, is still spreading at year 400.

Disturbance

Every year each cell has disturbance.chance of losing severity (default 0.8) of its trees and shrubs, as after windthrow or a fire. The cleared ground gets nearly full light, so the pioneers come back there first.

The field of the succession chart at year 400 with three disturbance chances. With a gap every 200 years in a cell the oak still holds the forest; with one every 50 years, birch and meadow mark most of it.

Anchors

Anchors of the rules file take part in two ways:

  • Plants (role: plant, properties: {species, crown}): a fixed plant, such as an old tree the story names. Its species covers the cells within crown metres of it or, when the crown is smaller than a cell, the share of the cell under it; the other species of its layer make room. It is mature from the start and stays every year.
  • Regions (role: region, an outline): areas the indicators measure.

Any anchor can be used in habitats as distance_to_<anchor id>, in metres, for plants that grow near something.

Managed land

Land people keep, such as fields and orchards: where its condition holds (written like a habitat, without soft edges), only the listed forms grow.

managed:
  - {where: {biome: [fields]}, forms: [herb]}
  - {where: {distance_to_old_oak: {max: 120}}, forms: [herb]}   # a clearing round a tree

The two managed lands above on a made-up field of 2.4 × 1.4 km in cells of 20 m, with an anchor that plants an old oak: herbs keep the fields and the clearing, and the forest closes everywhere else.

What it publishes

Output When Content
event vegetation start names, labels, forms, heights of the species
field vegetation start and every publish_every years the species that most marks every cell: the commonest tree where tree cover reaches 0.3, else the commonest shrub where shrubs reach 0.3, else the commonest herb where herbs reach 0.2; −1 for bare ground
fields tree_cover, shrub_cover, herb_cover the same share of the ground under each layer
fields cover_<id> the same, with species_fields: true cover of every species
event vegetation_indicators the listed years hectares of every species; hectares of forest, shrubland, grassland and bare ground; for every region, its forest share and its commonest species

Put the stage’s snapshots at multiples of publish_every, and keep only the fields worth saving with snapshot_fields: a large landscape’s fields take megabytes each.

Determinism and speed

The same seed gives the same landscape: the chance of a disturbance comes from a hash of the seed, the year and the cell, so cells can be worked on in parallel in any order. Cells are spread over the processors. A landscape of about 700,000 land cells and 18 species takes about a third of a second a year.

Known limits

  • Plants do not react yet to other plugins’ entities: a growing city does not clear the forest, and abandoned fields do not grow back.
  • The viewer does not draw fields yet; go-scene-builder can sow the vegetation from the vegetation field as a layer, as it does with biomes.
  • Seasons (flowering, leaves) belong to the climate’s seasons rule.

Tests

go test ./...

The tests grow synthetic landscapes and check what must hold anywhere: every species within its habitat, pioneers followed by shade-tolerant trees, disturbances keeping pioneers, anchored plants staying (and small crowns covering their share of a cell), managed land keeping forms out, soft habitat edges, and the same seed giving the same landscape.
