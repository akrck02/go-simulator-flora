🔍 How it works

How the plugin fits go-simulator

go-simulator is the host. It keeps the world (its fields, events and entities) and moves the clock. The flora plugin is a separate program the host starts (main.go serves it over standard input and output, go-simulator’s protocol).

  • At the start the plugin describes itself: its two rules, their parameters, what each one reads and what it writes. That description is also where the rules reference comes from.
  • vegetation reads the temperature, precipitation, elevation, slope and biome fields, the climate’s biomes event and the anchors, and writes the vegetation, tree_cover, shrub_cover and herb_cover fields (and cover_<id> when asked). Its instance name names the landscape.
  • vegetation_indicators reads nothing from other plugins: it measures the landscape of the vegetation rule it names and emits a vegetation_indicators event.

A plugin never touches the renderer: it reads its copy of the world and answers with changes (go-simulator’s writing plugins). Fields use physical units and shapes world units, as go-simulator’s vocabulary lists. The plugin reads what the terrain and climate plugins publish from the world instead of loading it again, so they must come before it in the rules file.

A step

The stage’s clock counts years. When the simulation starts, the vegetation rule lays the grid over the climate’s extent, samples the fields, works out how well every cell suits every species, plants the anchored plants and publishes the bare landscape. On every step it simulates the years the step covers, one at a time:

The seeds are spread first, for the whole landscape, with a separable Gaussian kernel (along the rows, then along the columns). Then every land cell grows, is disturbed and ages, from the state it had at the start of the year, so the cells are shared out over the processors. The fields are published again every publish_every years. The model has every formula.

The code

File What is in it
main.go starts the plugin
flora/plugin.go the two rules, setting up the landscape, anchors and regions
flora/landscape.go the grid and how the world’s fields are sampled on it
flora/habitat.go habitats, soft edges and distances to anchors
flora/species.go species, settings, managed land, disturbance
flora/succession.go one year: saplings, recruitment, death, room, disturbance, light
flora/dispersal.go the seed kernel and the parallel loop
flora/publish.go the fields, the names and the indicators

How the pictures were made

The maps and charts in these pages were drawn from the plugin’s own answers. A small throwaway program, kept outside the repository, ran the real plugin in a go-simulator host and read the fields it published after every year:

  • The example mountain (the README’s picture and the charts of its indicators): examples/valley.yaml with the real terrain and climate plugins, unchanged except for publish_every: 1 and species_fields: true, so every year could be drawn. Each cell shows the species of the vegetation field, paler where it covers less, shaded by the relief.
  • Spreading, succession, disturbance, habitat and managed land: small made-up flat fields with a stand-in for the climate that only names the biomes, and the scenarios written in the pictures’ captions. The tests use the same kind of synthetic landscape.

They are SVG drawings with their own light and dark colours, not screenshots: the viewer does not draw fields yet. The maps inside them are the cells of the published fields. The diagrams (how it fits, a cell, one year, the light) were drawn by hand in the same style; the cell in the landscape diagram is an example, not a measured one. The pictures of the made-up island in the model were in the docs before these pages; the scenario they come from is not in the repository.
